## Why this page exists

Every week, business owners in Kenya ask the same questions — in WhatsApp groups, business forums, and over the counter:

- *"I bought fuel for the delivery vehicle — is a till receipt from the pump enough?"*
- *"Can I claim my Safaricom internet as a business expense?"*
- *"The landlord won't give me any receipt — can I still claim rent?"*
- *"I pay KPLC tokens for the office — where's my eTIMS receipt?"*
- *"Do I need an eTIMS receipt for every single expense?"*

These are good questions, and most of the answers are shorter than you think. Here they are, one by one.

**Why it matters this month:** KRA now cross-checks declared expenses against eTIMS at filing — a receipt that never reached the system gets rejected, and your taxable profit grows. The receipts you collect today decide the return you file this year.

## The rule in one sentence

If you want to claim an expense, KRA must be able to trace it back to **your KRA PIN** — and for most purchases, that means a document that went through **eTIMS**.

A receipt without your PIN — or never passed through eTIMS — is treated as unproven: as if you guessed. Your profit looks bigger, and your tax bill is higher than it should be. That's where the phrase **"no eTIMS, no expense"** comes from, and it's accurate.

## Fuel — can you claim it without an eTIMS receipt?

Fuel is the classic case. If the vehicle is registered in the **business's name**, fuel for it is a genuine business expense — you claim it with a fuel receipt that went through eTIMS and carries your business PIN.

What doesn't work: generic till/M-Pesa receipts without your PIN, handwritten dockets, or "fuel" lines listed with no receipt.

Practical moves:
- **Ask for an eTIMS fuel receipt before you pay** — the fuel module is compulsory for stations and eTIMS receipts can be issued at the pump for both individual motorists and businesses. The deadline was extended to December 2025 and around 500 stations (about 16% of the country) were live by early 2026 — so a station that cannot issue yet is a station mid-onboarding.
- If a station still can't issue one, keep the normal receipt **plus** a record (date, litres, station, vehicle reg) in your books. Not perfect, but it's evidence.
- Ground rule: **the vehicle must be registered to the business.** A receipt for your personal car doesn't become the company's expense just because the business "used" it occasionally.

## Safaricom airtime and internet — can you claim them?

This is where owners get genuinely stuck — the mechanics exist but they're new.

The practical route:
- Safaricom runs an official KRA compliance flow on its business portal (business.safaricom.co.ke/kra) — you submit your business KRA PIN once, and Safaricom's invoices for your account are then issued with it and transmitted to iTax in real time. Customer care can walk you through the documentation.
- In parallel, **keep the payment records**: M-Pesa statements, the account number, any emailed invoice. If KRA validates and your PIN registration is pending, you at least have the audit trail built.

If you can't get the PIN on a specific token/airtime purchase, log it honestly and keep the payment proof.

## KPLC tokens and electricity — can you claim them?

Power is a large cost — and a gnarly one:

- Electricity billing is tied to the **meter owner's registration** — which is usually the landlord or the named account holder, not the person who buys tokens.
- KRA's own datasets include Kenya Power account records — so the meter's registered name should match the business that pays for it; that's exactly what makes a power expense traceable.
- If the meter is registered in **your business name**, the bills plus token purchase records are your evidence — and you're the party with the claim.
- If you buy tokens on someone else's meter (rented space, shared premises), the expense belongs to the meter owner, not the token payer. Don't "reverse-engineer" a fake receipt — fix it at setup instead: ask the owner to register the meter to you or issue you a tax invoice for the service.

## Rent — can you claim it without a landlord receipt?

- If your **landlord** is a VAT/eTIMS-compliant taxpayer, they should issue **monthly eTIMS rental invoices** that include your PIN. Landlords increasingly do this — KRA has been moving the rental sector onto e-invoices precisely so tenants can claim rent as a business cost: landlords earning between KES 288,000 and KES 15 million a year in rent sit in the Monthly Rental Income regime (7.5% on gross rent since January 2024) and that same landlord base is being onboarded onto eTIMS.
- If they refuse: **ask in writing**, keep every documented payment (M-Pesa reference, bank transfer), and hold the record. The claim without the invoice is the risky one — the ask-in-writing covers you in any audit conversation.

## M-Pesa till receipts

M-Pesa till receipts are **proof of payment**, not tax invoices. The supplier's eTIMS invoice (with your PIN) is the claimable document; the M-Pesa receipt proves you actually paid. Keep both — they serve different jobs.

## The onboarding error everyone meets: "wrong obligation ID" / "NONVAT only" — how to fix it today

During onboarding many owners see an error like *"wrong obligation ID"* or *"NONVAT only"* when they try to create an invoice in iTax. It's the single most common eTIMS problem for new users.

What's happening: the tax obligation on your PIN and the eTIMS registration aren't in sync — e.g. a NONVAT invoice type issued while no matching NONVAT obligation is active.

The practical fix flow:
1. Log in to iTax → confirm your obligations (VAT-registered? Income Tax – Business? TOT?)
2. Register the correct obligation and re-register your eTIMS account under that PIN
3. Issue using the invoice type that matches the error you saw
4. If it persists, raise the ticket with your obligation ID — this resolves in 1–2 working days

## FAQ — the exact questions owners search for

**Q: Do I need an eTIMS receipt for literally everything?**
For claims, yes — in practice KRA cross-references the big categories: supplier goods, fuel, rent, professional services, and imported digital services. The habit is right: always ask "can I get this as an eTIMS invoice with my PIN?" — from suppliers, supermarkets, fuel stations and providers.

**Q: Matatus and buses — is there an eTIMS receipt for business travel?**
A: Not systematically yet — but it's coming: ride-hailing (Uber, Bolt) fares have moved to eTIMS receipts in 2026. For matatu and bus trips, keep the tickets and records in a simple travel log — and expect this to change as the system extends.

**Q: Can I claim Facebook ads, Fiverr or an AI subscription without an eTIMS receipt?**
A: Foreign providers don't issue KRA eTIMS invoices. What you can do: hold the contract, the provider invoice, and the payment trail (M-Pesa/bank). Digital services handled through the digital-services tax regime follow their own rules — this area is moving; ask before claiming big amounts.

**Q: Must the vehicle be company-registered to claim fuel?**
Yes — fuel claims trace to the asset that's the business's. If you're a sole proprietor driving a personal car for client visits, consult how to structure the vehicle properly before stacking big fuel claims.
